The World Economic Forum (WEF) has become a symbol of an authoritarian economic system that is driving the world into wars, social crises, and ecological crises. Big Tech companies are establishing a new power bloc in Davos: Their business model is based on surveillance, injustice, and the exploitation of people and nature worldwide.

But resistance is growing: We’re organizing ourselves through grassroots democracy, building networks, and standing up to them on the protest march to Davos! Together, we stand for a socially just world with democratic self-governance and for global climate justice! Our protest and our organizational approach are inspired by the World Social Forum (WSF), which was founded in 2001 as an alternative to the WEF, opposes imperialism, and creates a space for mobilization, networking, and discussion.

The educational and protest trip to Davos brings together around 100 people to critically examine the role of the World Economic Forum and the growing influence of Big Tech on the economy, democracy, and the climate. Through workshops, international networking, and protest actions, we develop and advocate for shared perspectives on social justice, economic democracy, and a climate-just world.

Quick Facts

  • Topic: Big Tech, global inequality, and democratic alternatives
  • Date of the educational trip: January 14–18/19, 2027 (depending on the bus)
  • Solidarity price: €100–300
  • Pickup locations: Our Solibus departs from Berlin (GER) and stops in Leipzig (GER), Halle (GER), Nuremberg (GER), Memmingen (GER), and St. Gallen (CH)
Join us if you:
  • Want to share ideas and discuss Big Tech and how to fight it
  • Want to network with other activists and guests from the World Social Forum (WSF) in Benin
  • Want to protest against the World Economic Forum (WEF)
  • Want to travel together

No activism experience? We’re creating a space where even people who’ve never been politically active before but want to get involved can participate!

Webinar: From Data Centers to Mines: The Extractive Side of Digitalization (in English) + Presentation on the Educational Trip Optional
📅 Oct. 22, 2026 👥 Individual registration available 🕐 6:00 PM – 7:30 PM 💻 Online ▶ mehr Infos

Artificial intelligence often appears clean and intangible. Yet behind chatbots, the cloud, and data centers lie enormous amounts of energy, water, and raw materials. The expansion of Big Tech is driving up global demand for critical raw materials and exacerbating environmental destruction, human rights violations, and conflicts in mining regions.

In this webinar, together with Tiziana von Witzleben from Heiße Luft and Ben Hardmann from EarthRights, we’ll explore the material foundations of AI. We’ll discuss the role of rare earth elements and the consequences of their mining, particularly in Myanmar. We’ll also take a critical look at the expansion of data centers in Germany, their rising energy and water consumption, and political decisions that increasingly prioritize the interests of large technology corporations over environmental and public welfare concerns.

Organizational Concept

Support Groups: In your support group, you’ll organize yourselves independently and look out for one another during the educational trip and protest march. These can be your friends or people you meet for the first time during the trip. The support group concept has proven effective in political actions over the past few decades.

House Group: At our Swiss accommodation, we’ll be staying in several houses, so you’ll also be part of a house group that’s jointly responsible for meals and the respective house.

Working Groups: Our educational trip is largely organized on a grassroots, democratic basis. That’s why there are several working groups you can get involved in. Each working group is supervised by at least one person from StrikeWEF North. You don’t need any special skills to participate; you can learn them along the way. Almost all working groups will also collaborate with the structures of the Swiss alliance StrikeWEF.

The following working groups are available:

Goal: We want as many people as possible to come to Davos with us and stand up for a more just world.

Tasks: Among other things, designing and printing stickers, informing groups, and distributing flyers. This working group has little to do during the trip itself.

Goal: We want as many people as possible to learn about the problems with the WEF and what alternatives exist.

Task: Manage StrikeWEF’s social media channels—both during the mobilization and the march.

Goal: We want as many journalists and media outlets as possible to cover the protest in Davos and highlight our criticisms and demands.

Task: Research journalists, draft press narratives, write press releases, speak to the press if necessary (participate in the online press training on December 7 from 6:00 p.m. to 9:00 p.m.), or support those speaking to the press.

Goal: We want to draw attention to our goals through (creative) actions.

Task: Plan actions related to the protest march, involve participants, and carry out the actions. This working group must begin its work quite early.

Goal: We want the educational trip to be as safe a space as possible for all participants, to prevent discrimination as much as possible, and to address it in a transformative way should it occur.

Task: Develop an awareness concept, compile materials, define shifts, track staffing, and be available to answer questions from awareness coordinators.

Goal: To effectively integrate our guests from outside Europe into the group and support them throughout the entire trip. We also want to ensure there is meaningful interaction with them during the educational trip.

Responsibilities: Assist with visa matters, discuss wishes, expectations, and concerns, organize the stay in Germany and Switzerland, and arrange for translation and support during the educational trip.

Goal: We want to manage our own arrangements at our accommodations in St. Gallen across all four houses.

Responsibilities: Familiarize yourself with the schedule, be approachable to roommates, create shift schedules, and ensure that we leave the houses clean.

Tickets & Costs

When you register, you’ll receive our bank account information and can pay your participation fee. We’ll then send you the invoice and ticket number afterward. Only then will you have a confirmed ticket.

The price includes transportation, lodging, and meals. Please bring snacks for the bus ride and the hike, though.

Sliding Scale: Here’s our solidarity-based pricing system. We’d like to make solidarity spots and travel expenses available to people who are part of political groups in the Global South. If you don’t have any money right now, don’t let that stop you—just indicate in your registration that you need a solidarity spot.

  • €100.00 solidarity spot
  • €200.00 cost-covering
  • €300.00 solidarity price (funds a solidarity spot)

By the way, it’s so affordable because many people are organizing the trip without pay. The solidarity buses are also driven by activists.

Accessibility (and accessibility improvements)

  • Each bus has one wheelchair-accessible seat.
  • The accommodations in Switzerland are not fully accessible. The dormitory has an accessible bathroom and dining room (door width at least 73.5 cm), but there are three steps leading to the bedroom.
  • The lodging offers single, double, and shared rooms. If you need a single room or a double room with a close friend—for example, to have a private space—please indicate this on the registration form.
  • During the hike, we’ll be staying in an accessible, heated gymnasium. This means that a large number of people will be sleeping in one room.
  • The bus ride from Berlin to the lodging and back takes about 13 hours. There is no restroom on the bus. Breaks can be taken as needed.
  • Individual sections of the hike can be skipped by taking the train if walking is too challenging.
  • Most of the program is in German, but some parts are also in English. During in-person events, whispered translation into the other language can be arranged.
  • We can accommodate food intolerances. We’ll ask about these before the trip.
  • Children have joined the hike in recent years (please email us if you’d like to bring children). Childcare is not provided.
  • Unfortunately, people under 18 are not permitted to participate without a legal guardian.

Please email us with any requests or needs. We strive to break down barriers.

FAQs

How does communication within the group work? We’ll communicate via the Signal messenger in an internal group. That’s why we’ll ask for your Signal contact information on the registration form.

How many kilometers will we hike? Once the route is finalized, it will be posted on a separate subpage. It’s important to note that no special fitness level is required, and if the hike becomes too strenuous, you can take the regional train part of the way.

It’s cold in winter—what temperatures should I expect? That’s hard to predict. In our experience, daytime temperatures are usually around 0°C. In recent years, it’s often been sunny.

Will there be snow? So far, there’s always been snow. The depth has varied between 20 and 80 cm.

What should I pack? A customized packing list for the educational trip will be sent to all registered participants before departure.
